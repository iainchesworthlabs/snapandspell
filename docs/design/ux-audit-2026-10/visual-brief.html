The verdict

The engine is ready.
The product surface is the work.

The game underneath is real: a fast solver, a thoughtful privacy model, generous targets and a genuinely good scan-and-confirm flow. But the first screen is wordless, the board is a 3D scene pasted next to empty cards, the jewels are almost invisible, and the parent area is a second product. This is not a palette problem. It needs a design system, a clear split between what a child does and what a grown-up does, and a feedback loop that makes a five-year-old feel clever.

Evidence · the first thirty seconds

A child sees two pictures. A parent sees a puzzle.

The home screen is the product's front door. It has no wordmark, no app name, no tagline and no visible mode labels. The two largest choices are a grid and a monitor.

A five-year-old

“Which one is play?”

Two wordless cards. One looks like a grid, one like a monitor. No play triangle, no hand, no spoken label until after the choice. The child has to guess, or ask an adult. The adult has no visible instructions either.

A parent

“Where are the settings?”

The family mode looks like a monitor, not a camera or a real board. Choosing it leads to a no-camera screen and a 16-cell typing form. The dashboard, sound and language are behind an unlabelled person icon and a two-circle gate.

The role problem. Solo Play is a child's game. The Family Companion is an adult-led setup: place a board, capture it, check 16 letters, confirm. The home treats them as two equal child choices and hides the adult's settings. That is upside down.
The findings

Seven things a design director would stop the line for.

Each finding is a product problem, not a preference. The evidence and the code cause are in the full audit; the fix is in the direction below.

01 / CRITICAL
Critical

No identity, no design system.

No wordmark, no app icon, no bundled typeface, no single theme, no shared app bar. The title bar shows a generic document icon; the child kit is square and outline-only; the parent kit is rounded and filled; the 3D scene has its own colours.

Fix: one wordmark, one icon, one type system, one palette, one component kit. The parent area is the same product, denser.
Critical
02

The home is a wordless, mis-targeted mode picker.

Two huge unlabelled pictures ask a pre-reader to choose between a game and an adult scanning workflow. The good names — “Play on my own”, “Play with the family” — exist only for the screen reader.

Fix: label the cards, make Play primary, label For grown-ups, and put the adult workflow behind the gate.
Critical
03

The board and the panel are disconnected.

The box is invisible on the review build. The board sits on a slate 3D deck that ignores the theme. The panel is three blank cards. The 3D seams and perspective fight the letters.

Fix: a framed board stage; a flat, crisp play surface; titled panel cards with empty states.
High
04

The loop has no visible reward or ending.

A found word adds a small list row. Clearing the board produces a glow, then a three-second move to a hidden hold-to-play screen. There is no “you found all the words”, no jewel summary, no praise.

Fix: a jewel for every word, a spoken word, a clear summary, visible Play again and Home.
High
05

Child controls are icon-only and often silent.

Four grids, a ring-and-dot, a person, a gem with a tick. Only three of 29 buttons set the spoken label. A five-year-old cannot decode the interface; a parent cannot help.

Fix: a visible word and a spoken word on every child control; test the icons with children.
High
06

The parent area is a second product.

Rounded buttons, 12 px radii and a white bar in the parent area; square outlines in the child area. Settings are one unbroken list ordered by implementation. The dashboard empty state is a dead end.

Fix: one design system; five settings groups; a welcome card; privacy at the top; a compact gate.
High
07

The app is not productised.

Fixed 1100 × 700 window, top-left, no persistence, no loading state, no icon, Andika named but not bundled, no line between the OS frame and the app.

Fix: centre and remember the window; branded first paint; bundle the fonts; a real app icon; a 2D board fallback.
The house comparison

Same house. Different discipline.

Countdown Solver and ICL Forge do not look alike, but they share a discipline: a wordmark, a type system, one accent, labelled controls, clear hierarchy and real empty states. WordJewels has the opposite profile.

Borrow

Wordmark, hierarchy, labels, empty states.

Countdown's page title and calm cards; Forge's editorial sections, mono labels, one accent and explanatory notes. Adapt them for a child: bigger, warmer, spoken.

Do not borrow

Sidebar, density, adult tone.

A five-year-old does not need a navigation rail, a 1280 × 900 minimum or a 16 px settings row. Child-sized does not mean wordless.

The direction

A jewel box
on the table.

Warm paper, a deep tray, cream letter dice, an emerald accent and a gold reward. Flat editorial chrome around a tactile, physical play object. One product for the child and the parent, with the adult work behind the gate.

One obvious next step.One primary action, labelled and reachable, on every screen.
Show, say, spell.A picture, a word and a voice; never an icon alone for a child.
The board is the hero.Frame it, light it, and settle it flat for reading and tracing.
A jewel for every win.Visible, cumulative, satisfying; no score, no streak, no pressure.
Calm, not empty.Every card has a title, a purpose and an empty state.
Grown-up jobs behind the gate.Scanning, typing, settings and progress are adult workflows.

The plan

Four phases from prototype to product.

Ordered by user risk. Phase 0 removes the prototype signals and makes a child test possible; Phase 1 builds the design system and the loop; Phase 2 makes the family and parent workflows real; Phase 3 validates and polishes.

PHASE 0

Stop the prototype look

3–5 days

Wordmark, icon, home labels, panel titles and empty states, visible Done, path fix, clear summary, parent welcome, centred window, 2D fallback.

PHASE 1

One system + the child loop

2–3 weeks

Tokens, type, components, home, Solo box, flat board, titled panel, reward loop, gate and dashboard v1.

PHASE 2

Family + parent depth

3–4 weeks

Scan stepper, check screen, picker dialog, assist, parent onboarding, settings groups, content and recordings.

PHASE 3

Validate + release polish

2 weeks

5 children + 5 parents, accessibility, visual QA, performance, packaging, store assets.

Minimum lovable slice. If the full overhaul is too much to start: identity + home labels + a visible box + a flat board + titled panel cards + a visible Done + a clear summary. That is enough to test whether a five-year-old can start, make a word, understand what happened, and stop.
The next decisions

Five choices unlock the work.

The full audit has the screen-by-screen evidence, the code causes, the token tables, the validation script and the appendices. These are the decisions I need from you.

  1. Choose the role model.One primary Play card with the adult workflows behind the gate (recommended), or two labelled cards with the family card gated. This changes the home.
  2. Approve the visual direction.Jewel Box: warm paper, deep tray, emerald accent, gold reward, Archivo + Andika + Plex Mono, 12 px soft-modernist. Or a stricter Forge-like direction.
  3. Commit to the play surface.Flat 2D for reading and tracing, 3D for the shake and drop. If 3D stays for play, it must become orthographic, seam-free and token-driven.
  4. Name the design owner.A living style guide and one reviewer for every new screen. Otherwise the fourth vocabulary appears next sprint.
  5. Book the children.Five 5–6 year olds and five parents after Phase 0/1. The biggest risk is not the solver; it is that the interface assumes a user who can already read it.
What I would do first

A one-week clarity pass.

Add a wordmark and app icon. Label the home and every child control. Put “Words I found” and an empty state on the panel. Move Done into a visible action zone. Fix the path. Add the clear summary. Centre the window. Then put it in front of one child and one parent before touching the 3D scene again.
